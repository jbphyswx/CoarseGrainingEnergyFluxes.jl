Theory

Coarse-Graining Framework

The coarse-grained (filtered) field at scale ℓ is a convolution with a normalized kernel G_ℓ:

ū_ℓ(x) = ∫ G_ℓ(x, x') u(x') dA(x')

G_ℓ is normalized to unit mass over all space, so a constant field filters to itself. The default ZeroFill strategy filters the field extended by zero over land and past a bounded edge — land is fluid at rest (Aluie et al. 2018; Grooms et al. 2021, eq. 7) — with G_ℓ unchanged: the denominator is the kernel's mass over the lattice continued past each bounded edge at its edge spacing, a latitude to the pole and a regional longitude around the ring, and for the top-hat weighing cells by their overlap the measure of its support itself. That preserves the property the rest of this page depends on: a position-independent kernel commutes with spatial derivatives, which is the step that turns the pointwise momentum equation into the filtered energy budget below. ū_ℓ and Π are therefore defined over land too, Π vanishing beyond the kernel's reach of the coast, and a domain mean sums every cell and divides by the water area (Storer et al. 2022). The alternative Deformable strategy renormalizes over the locally-active area and zeroes masked cells, recovering constants exactly next to a boundary at the cost of that commutation; see Filtering.filter_field!.

Sub-Scale Stress Tensor

The sub-filter-scale (SFS) stress captures the effect of motions smaller than ℓ:

τ_ℓ = (u ⊗ u)̄_ℓ − ū_ℓ ⊗ ū_ℓ

It is the local, scale-resolved analogue of the Reynolds stress.

Cross-Scale Energy Flux

The filtered kinetic-energy budget (Aluie 2011; Aluie, Hecht & Vallis 2018) contains the cross-scale flux

Π_ℓ(x) = −τ_ℓ : S̄_ℓ = −Σᵢⱼ τᵢⱼ S̄ᵢⱼ ,   S̄ = ½(∇ū + (∇ū)ᵀ)

per unit mass, in m² s⁻³; ρ₀ Π_ℓ is the flux per unit volume.

  • Π > 0 — forward cascade (energy from large → small scales)
  • Π < 0 — inverse cascade (small → large)
  • ⟨Π⟩ > 0 — net forward cascade across the domain at scale ℓ

In 2D the contraction is S_xx τ_xx + 2 S_xy τ_xy + S_yy τ_yy; in 3D it gains + S_zz τ_zz + 2 S_xz τ_xz + 2 S_yz τ_yz (see Diagnostics.compute_Π!).

Derivatives of the filtered field

S̄ differentiates ū, which every filter defines at every point. Every diagnostic that differentiates a filtered field takes derivative (Derivatives.AbstractDerivative):

  • PhysicalDerivative(; nodes = 5) differences ū's values: FlowGeometries' stencils of nodes nodes on a StructuredGrid, a window that reaches an inactive cell shrinking to the cell's active run, and its least-squares tangent-plane gradient on every other grid. A centred stencil of n nodes is of order n − 1, so its error on ū, smooth on the scale ℓ, falls as (Δ/ℓ)^{n−1}.
  • SpectralDerivative() differentiates the filter's own output. A spectral filter's output is the series ū(x) = Σ_k w_k Ĝ(k) F̂(k) e^{ik·x} (w_k splitting each Nyquist pair), and Σ_k w_k i k_d Ĝ(k) F̂(k) e^{ik·x} is its derivative, exact for that series; a renormalized filter ū = N/M takes the quotient rule ∂ū = (∂N − ū ∂M)/M. Under the generalized convolution the strain comes from the filtered spin-1 coefficients through one spin-0 and one spin-2 synthesis, the spin-lowering and raising operators scaling a degree-ℓ coefficient by √(ℓ(ℓ+1))/R and √((ℓ−1)(ℓ+2))/R; under CartesianComponents each ambient component's surface gradient is a spin-1 synthesis of its scalar coefficients scaled by √(ℓ(ℓ+1))/R, rotated to the local frame. A plan with no series raises.
  • AutoDerivative(), the default, takes the spectral derivative where the caller filters spectrally (method = Spectral(), and the generalized convolution) and PhysicalDerivative() elsewhere, whichever engine evaluates a RealSpace() filter.

enstrophy_flux takes the vorticity of the unfiltered velocity by differences under every route, since only a filtered field is a series; the gradient of the filtered vorticity follows the route.

What a Π-only diagnostic does not tell you

Π is the cross-scale transfer term of the filtered kinetic-energy budget, one term of several. Under exact homogeneity the transport term ⟨∇·J⟩ averages to zero; it is the only other term that drops out. Baroclinic conversion and forcing/injection are not divergences and do not vanish under averaging.

Loose et al. (2023) report cross-scale KE transfer as 35–40% of baroclinic EKE production in the eddy-permitting regime, 70–100% at some latitudes, and >100% locally. So Π supports statements like "at this scale and place, energy moves upscale", and does not by itself attribute the mesoscale energy to upscale transfer: weighing it against baroclinic instability needs the other terms, which this package does not compute.

The large-scale energy is E(ℓ) = ½⟨|ū_ℓ|²⟩, the energy of the filtered flow, which Diagnostics.cumulative_energy computes. The filtered energy ½⟨(|u|²)‾_ℓ⟩ is a different quantity and does not obey the cascade budget.

Filter Kernels

What the framework requires of a kernel

The results above hold for a kernel that is:

  1. Normalized, ∫G dA = 1, so a constant filters to itself and ū is a local mean.
  2. Even (G(-x) = G(x)), so ∫xG = 0 and filtering does not displace the field. A kernel with a non-zero first moment advects while it smooths, and gives every linear field a spurious τ.
  3. Position-independent, so filtering commutes with ∇. Commutation turns the pointwise momentum equation into the filtered budget, and the default masking strategy ZeroFill preserves it; see Filtering.filter_field!.
  4. Non-negative and of non-vanishing second moment, if Π is to be interpreted as a local energy transfer. A kernel with ∫x²G = 0 (a "high-order" kernel) improves spectrum fidelity and takes negative values, which breaks realizability of τ: the flux and the spectrum ask opposite things of the kernel here.

Requirements 1 and 2 hold by construction: every engine divides by the window mass, and every kernel is a function of distance or, for HighOrderKernel on the plane, a product of even profiles. Requirement 3 is gated by the filter ∘ ∇ == ∇ ∘ filter test (test_filtering.jl), and 4 by the sign checks of test_kernels.jl. TopHatKernel and GaussianKernel satisfy all four. SharpSpectralKernel satisfies 1–3 but its real-space form takes negative values, so it fails 4 — usable for the spectrum, questionable for a pointwise Π.

Scale convention

The filter scale ℓ is the full filter width, a diameter (Pope 2000). The top-hat spans the disk/ball of radius ℓ/2; the sharp-spectral cutoff sits at k_c = π/ℓ. FlowSieve uses the same convention, so an ℓ is directly comparable; a source that reports a radius or a Gaussian standard deviation is not, and needs converting first. Real-space weights are unnormalized — the filtering routines divide by the running area/volume-weighted sum.

Top-Hat (box) — TopHatKernel

Unit weight inside the disk/ball of radius ℓ/2, zero outside. The literature default (Aluie et al. 2018, Storer et al. 2022). Spectral filtering needs using SpecialFunctions to load the exact planar transfer function (Bessel J₁); see below.

On a grid the top-hat's sum over cells weighs each cell as cells says (Kernels.AbstractCellWeight). The default, CellOverlap(), weighs a cell by its measure inside the support, the continuous top-hat integrated over the cell, so the filtered field varies continuously with ℓ and with the target's position. The support is the segment, disk or ball of a Cartesian grid, the spherical cap on a sphere, the geodesic disk on a spheroid, and the ball of chord radius ℓ/2 in a spherical or spheroidal shell. Each fraction is a closed form (a box or a polygon against a disk) or a Gauss–Legendre quadrature of one between the exact breakpoints of the integrand. CellCentre() weighs a cell by its whole measure where its centre lies within ℓ/2 and by nothing otherwise, so the filter steps as ℓ crosses the cell centres. A node set, whose points carry no cells, takes the centre test under either.

A top-hat's Π on a grid converges to the continuous filter's at first order in Δ/ℓ under either rule: the kernel's edge passes grid-scale content into ū. The centre test's steps add to that error at small ℓ/Δ, and dominate the filtering spectrum there, which differentiates E(ℓ) in ℓ. A smooth kernel's Π converges faster than any power of Δ/ℓ on a uniform grid.

Gaussian — GaussianKernel(; α = 6)

G_ℓ(r) ∝ exp(−α (r/ℓ)²)
  • α = 6 (default) is the Pope/turbulence convention: per-component variance σ² = ℓ²/(2α) = ℓ²/12, the second moment of a top-hat of width ℓ in one dimension.
  • α = 4 reproduces FlowSieve's Gaussian, so GaussianKernel(; α = 4) is directly comparable to FlowSieve output.

Variance matching is dimension-dependent. The Gaussian is separable, so its per-component variance is ℓ²/(2α) in every dimension. The top-hat is the disk/ball of radius R = ℓ/2, whose per-component variance R²/(D+2) shrinks with the dimension D:

top-hat ⟨x²⟩ℓ²/12ℓ²/16ℓ²/20

So the default α = 6 is 15% wider in RMS than a 2-D top-hat at the same nominal ℓ. For a like-for-like kernel comparison use GaussianKernel(; α = 8) in 2-D and α = 10 in 3-D.

Sharp Spectral — SharpSpectralKernel

Ideal low-pass: Ĝ_ℓ(k) = 1 for k ≤ k_c = π/ℓ, else 0. method = Spectral() multiplies by it. RealSpace() convolves with its inverse transform in the grid's D dimensions,

G(r) ∝ J_{D/2}(k_c r) / (k_c r)^{D/2}:    sin x / x,   2 J₁(x)/x,   3 (sin x − x cos x)/x³,    x = k_c r,

for D = 1, 2, 3 (the two-dimensional form needs using SpecialFunctions). G decays as r^{−(D+1)/2}, so ∫|G| diverges and no finite footprint reproduces the brick wall: the truncated kernel's transfer function ripples about it, most near k_c, with an amplitude falling as 1/(|k_c − k| R) in the truncation radius R. The footprint ends near 10ℓ, at the root of the truncated mass, (2/π) Si(k_c R) = 1, J₀(k_c R) = 0 or (2/π)(Si(k_c R) − sin k_c R) = 1, so the normalized passband is centred on 1. Its weights change sign, so under Deformable a window mass beside a coast can approach zero.

Spectral transfer functions

For spectral filtering, each mode of wavenumber magnitude k is multiplied by Kernels.spectral_transfer(kernel, k, ℓ), normalized so Ĝ(0) = 1 (the mean is preserved):

KernelĜ(k, ℓ)
GaussianKernel(α)exp(−k² ℓ² / 4α)
SharpSpectralKernel1 if k ≤ π/ℓ, else 0
TopHatKernel2 J₁(kR)/(kR), R = ℓ/2 (needs using SpecialFunctions)
HighOrderKernel∏_d Ĝ₁(k_d) over the wavevector, Ĝ₁(k) = Σₙ cₙ 2ρₙ sinc(kρₙ) / Σₙ cₙ 2ρₙ with ρₙ = ℓ/2 + nb and cₙ = aₙ − aₙ₊₁

Along a bounded direction the spectral filter is, as the real-space one is, the convolution of the field extended by zero. The transform pads the direction until the kernel at the largest scale it serves (max_scale of Filtering.spectral_grid_plan, the largest of a sweep's scales) stays clear of the domain's periodic copies, and a plan applied at a larger scale raises. Where a kernel's transfer at the grid's Nyquist wavenumber is not negligible (a Gaussian below ℓ ≈ 8Δ), the lattice cuts it and leaves a slowly decaying tail in real space, so the result depends on the padding by about Ĝ(π/Δ)/(π g) for a gap of g cells.

The sharp kernel reaches without bound, so no padding serves it. On a uniform grid of two directions the FFT path filters by the lattice brick-wall kernel over the offsets the domain holds, whose transform on the padded box is exact: with both directions bounded h(o) = ΔxΔy k_c²/(4π) · 2J₁(k_c r)/(k_c r), r = |o|, and with one periodic, per wavenumber k of the periodic direction h(o) = Δ sin(a oΔ)/(π oΔ) along the bounded one, a = √(k_c² − k²), zero where |k| > k_c. It needs ℓ ≥ Δ along a bounded direction. A nonuniform-FFT plan with a bounded direction raises for the sharp kernel.

On a sphere of radius R a radial kernel multiplies every harmonic of degree l by its Legendre transform (Aluie 2019, eq. 34, normalized as eq. 10; the Funk–Hecke theorem, eqs. 36–37), taken over the support the real-space engines keep:

Ĝ(l) = ∫₀^γₜ G(Rγ) P_l(cos γ) sin γ dγ / ∫₀^γₜ G(Rγ) sin γ dγ ,   γₜ = min(kernel_radius(kernel, ℓ, Val(2))/R, π) .

Kernels.spectral_transfer_degrees! fills Ĝ(0 … L) at once. A spherical cap (TopHatKernel, and each shell of HighOrderKernel's isotropic form, weighted by its mass) has the closed form of Jekeli (1981), Ĝ(l) = (1 + x)P′_l(x)/(l(l+1)) with x = cos γₜ; every other kernel is integrated by Gauss–Legendre quadrature in γ, with enough points to integrate G(Rγ)P_l(cos γ) sin γ to round-off for every l ≤ L. Both run in the element type of the transfer, the grid's. The spectral filter on a sphere is thus the transform of the real-space filter's own kernel: SharpSpectralKernel there is the Legendre transform of its truncated inverse transform, which ripples about the brick wall as on the plane, and SmoothHatKernel and HyperGaussianKernel, which have no closed planar transform, have a degree transfer.

The Filtering Spectrum (Sadek & Aluie 2018)

Filtering at a continuum of scales yields a spectrum without windowing or periodicity assumptions. The cumulative coarse-grained kinetic energy (Diagnostics.cumulative_energy, their Eq. 15) is

E(ℓ) = ½ ⟨|ū_ℓ|²⟩ ,

per unit mass, a cumulative quantity. The filtering spectral density (Diagnostics.filtering_spectrum, their Eq. 14 — comparable to a Fourier energy spectrum) is its derivative with respect to the filtering wavenumber k_ℓ = L/ℓ:

Ẽ(k_ℓ) = d/dk_ℓ [ ½ ⟨|ū_ℓ|²⟩ ] .

L is the region length (L = 1 gives the FlowSieve convention k_ℓ = 1/ℓ).

The k_ℓ = C/ℓ convention is not standardized

Three mappings are all in use, and they are not interchangeable:

sourcemappingreached here by
Sadek & Aluie (2018)k_ℓ = L/ℓ, L the domain sizeL = domain size
Storer et al. (2022, 2023), FlowSievek_ℓ = 1/ℓL = 1 (the default)
Rivera, Aluie & Ecke (2014)k_ℓ = 2π/ℓL = 2π

Under k_ℓ = C/ℓ the Jacobian is dℓ/dk_ℓ = -ℓ²/C, so k_ℓ scales as C and the density Ẽ scales as 1/C. Comparing amplitudes — or peak locations — against a Fourier spectrum or against another code therefore means nothing unless the conventions have been matched first. The cumulative E(ℓ) is convention-free; only the density is not.

Two limits on the density

  • Slope ceiling. Sadek & Aluie's Eq. 18: a kernel with p vanishing moments recovers a true k^{-α} spectrum only while α < p + 2, and otherwise saturates at k^{-(p+2)}. TopHatKernel and GaussianKernel both have p = 1, so the recovered slope locks at k⁻³ — which is precisely the 2-D enstrophy-range target slope, so this bites hardest in QG work. Π is unaffected; the ceiling is a property of the spectrum diagnostic alone.
  • Positive-definiteness. Ẽ(k_ℓ) ≥ 0 is guaranteed (their Eq. 21) only when d|Ĝ(k)|²/dk ≤ 0 on (0, ∞). The Gaussian and sharp-spectral kernels satisfy it; the top-hat does not — Ĝ = 2J₁(kR)/(kR) falls to zero at kℓ ≈ 7.66 and its |Ĝ|² then climbs back to 0.0175 at kℓ ≈ 10.27, the first Airy sidelobe, with later ones at 0.0042 and 0.0016. The condition is sufficient but not necessary, and the violation here is under 2% of the DC value and confined to the far sub-filter tail. By default Diagnostics.filtering_spectrum throws for a non-conforming kernel and coarse_grain fills its density with NaN; Diagnostics.ForceSpectrum() computes the density anyway and leaves the sign check to the caller. See Diagnostics.AbstractSpectrumPolicy and Kernels.transfer_monotone.

The derivative in ℓ

E(ℓ) = ½⟨|ū_ℓ|²⟩ gives dE/dℓ = ⟨ū_ℓ · ∂_ℓ ū_ℓ⟩ exactly, and Ẽ(k_ℓ) = −(ℓ²/L) dE/dℓ. scale_derivative (Diagnostics.AbstractScaleDerivative) chooses how ∂_ℓ ū is taken:

  • ExactScaleDerivative() through the filter itself. A spectral filter or the generalized convolution synthesizes with the transfer ∂_ℓĜ, and a renormalized one takes the quotient rule over its mask's series. A real-space filter is ū = N_G/M_G, N_G = Σ g(r/ℓ) u A and M_G its window mass, so with H(s) = −s g′(s) (Kernels.ScaleRate), ∂_ℓ ū = (M_H/M_G)(ū_H − ū)/ℓ, ū_H the filter of kernel H over the same taps: the derivative of the discrete filter as it is evaluated.
  • DifferenceScaleDerivative(), the slope at each scale of the quadratic through the three nearest scales' E, second order in the scale spacing.
  • AutoScaleDerivative(), the default: exact where the kernel has the derivative on the filter's route, and differences elsewhere. The Gaussian, smooth hat and hyper-Gaussian have it in real space; the Gaussian, top-hat and HighOrderKernel in a planar spectral filter; the Gaussian, smooth hat, hyper-Gaussian and top-hat by degree on a sphere. The top-hat's H is a ring at its rim, HighOrderKernel's sits at its edges and the sharp kernel's ∂_ℓĜ is a delta, so their real-space footprints take differences.

With slopes = Slopes() a sweep also returns the spectrum's slope d ln Ẽ/d ln k_ℓ at each scale. With a = ℓ∂_ℓū and b = ℓ²∂²_ℓū it is −(2⟨ū·a⟩ + ⟨a·a⟩ + ⟨ū·b⟩)/⟨ū·a⟩ on an exact route, the second derivative taken as the first is, and the slope of the quadratic through the three nearest scales' ln Ẽ in ln k_ℓ otherwise. spectrum_maps = SpectrumMaps() keeps the local spectrum Ẽ(x, k_ℓ) = −(ℓ/L) ū·a at every cell (shaped like Π, its area mean the filtering spectrum) and, with Slopes(), its slope per cell. Filtering.plan_filter_sweep(…; scale_rates = ScaleRates()) builds a real-space family's rate plans with it, for a sweep repeated over timesteps.

Decompositions

Leonard / Cross / Reynolds — tau_decomposition

Germano's (1992) split of the stress into generalized central moments, each individually Galilean invariant, with L + C + R = τ exactly: resolved–resolved (Leonard), resolved–subfilter (Cross), and subfilter–subfilter (Reynolds, the backscatter-carrying term). On a sphere tau_decomposition filters the velocity and every product tensor by the vector filter compute_Π! uses (Filtering vectors and tensors on the sphere); each filter is linear, so the three moments sum to that filter's τ and come back in local east/north components.

Rotational / divergent (Helmholtz) — compute_Π_decomposed

Given the rotational (solenoidal) velocity uʳ (from a Helmholtz solver such as HelmholtzDecomposition.jl; the divergent part uᵈ = u - uʳ is the complement), a both-sided split is required for a physically complete decomposition: the strain as well as the stress, since S̄ = S̄ʳ + S̄ᵈ in general. Splitting only the stress against the full, undecomposed strain (Π ≟ -S̄:τʳʳ - S̄:τ_cross - S̄:τᵈᵈ) drops the S̄ᵈ:τʳʳ and S̄ʳ:τᵈᵈ cross-terms whenever the divergent strain is non-negligible, leaving the decomposition incomplete (Wang et al.'s extension of Aluie's framework; Barkan, Srinivasan & McWilliams 2024). The stress itself is also bilinear in the two velocity parts, τ = τʳʳ + τᵈᵈ + τ_X (τ_X the rotational–divergent cross stress), so contracting both sides in full gives three exact channels:

Π_RR = −S̄ʳ : τʳʳ                                          (rotational → rotational)
Π_DD = −S̄ᵈ : τᵈᵈ                                          (divergent  → divergent)
Π_X  = −( S̄ʳ:τᵈᵈ + S̄ᵈ:τʳʳ + S̄ʳ:τ_X + S̄ᵈ:τ_X )              (every interaction term)

Π = Π_RR + Π_X + Π_DD   exactly, to machine precision

compute_Π_decomposed returns (; total, rotational, cross, divergent) with total = rotational .+ cross .+ divergent; the three channels hold Π_RR, Π_X, and Π_DD respectively. Π_X is the "stimulated cascade" / interaction channel of Barkan, Srinivasan & McWilliams (2024) — energy exchanged between the rotational and divergent parts of the flow, which the one-sided (stress-only) split cannot represent at all.

Strain / convergence — compute_Π_strain_convergence

Srinivasan, Barkan & McWilliams (2023) eq. (10) splits the same Π by diagonalizing the filtered strain tensor. With the rotation invariants δ̄ = ū_x + v̄_y (divergence) and ᾱ = √(σ̄_n² + σ̄_s²) (strain magnitude), built from the normal and shear strains σ̄_n = ū_x − v̄_y and σ̄_s = ū_y + v̄_x,

Π = Π_α − Π_δ ,   Π_α = (τ_vv − τ_uu) σ̄_n/2 − τ_uv σ̄_s ,   Π_δ = (τ_vv + τ_uu) δ̄/2

Π_α is deformation/shear production — present in non-divergent flow, and the only term when δ̄ = 0, which recovers Polzin (2010). Π_δ is convergence production, the frontogenetic term that paper adds; it vanishes identically for a non-divergent field.

Expanding eq. (10) collapses exactly to −τ_uu ū_x − τ_uv(ū_y + v̄_x) − τ_vv v̄_y, the direct −S̄:τ̄, so the split adds no new information. Its value is (a) the physical reading — Π binned against δ̄ and ᾱ, which the function also returns — and (b) that the two forms contract different combinations of the same derivatives, so agreeing to round-off is a real check on both. The suite asserts that agreement on masked and unmasked grids, for both kernels and both mask strategies.

Tracer / buoyancy variance flux — tracer_variance_flux

The scalar analogue of Π for a tracer θ (Aluie & Eyink):

Πθ = −∂_j θ̄ · τ_j(u, θ) ,   τ_j = ⟨u_j θ⟩ − ū_j θ̄ .

With θ = buoyancy this is the cross-scale buoyancy-variance (APE-related) transfer, needing only (u, v, θ).

τ_j is a vector, so on a spherical grid u and uθ are filtered by the vector filter of the momentum stress (Filtering vectors and tensors on the sphere), θ̄ by the same transforms at spin 0, and τ is contracted with ∂_j θ̄ in the local east/north frame. In a spherical shell τ is formed in ambient Cartesian components and rotated to the local east/north/radial frame.

Vertical structure: vertical-profile vs. true 3D

In 2.5D, compute_Π! omits the vertical-shear strain terms (S_xz, S_yz, S_zz are omitted or zero) whenever only (u, v) (optionally w) is supplied on a 2D grid. This is the standard thin-layer/quasi-geostrophic scaling (Vallis, Atmospheric and Oceanic Fluid Dynamics; Pedlosky, Geophysical Fluid Dynamics), valid when the aspect ratio δ = H/L (vertical/horizontal scale) is small — the normal regime for large-scale ocean/atmosphere flow, where vertical shear is subdominant to horizontal gradients. The "vertical structure via coarse-graining" literature (Aluie, Hecht & Vallis 2018; the Buzzicotti/Storer/Khatri/Griffies/Aluie line of work) runs this same 2D/2.5D method independently at each vertical level of a multi-level model and compares/stacks the resulting profiles. coarse_grain_profile implements this: given 3D (x, y, z) arrays it treats the vertical axis as a batch over the shared horizontal grid, sweeping the 2D/2.5D coarse_grain! independently per level and returning the stacked profile; the level axis parallelizes like any other batch axis.

compute_Π! also has a coupled true 3D method (StructuredGrid{...,3}, Cartesian or spherical-volumetric): all nine strain/stress components, vertical derivatives, and one filter kernel spanning all three directions. It targets homogeneous/isotropic turbulence (e.g. Rayleigh–Taylor or boundary-layer studies), where the thin-layer assumption does not hold and levels interact through the filter.

Spherical Geometry

On S² of radius R, convolution uses the great-circle distance

d(x, x') = R · arccos(sin φ sin φ' + cos φ cos φ' cos(λ − λ')) ,

with area element dA = R² cos φ dλ dφ. The true 3D spherical-volumetric StructuredGrid (a (lon, lat, radius) axis triple whose third axis is the radius itself) uses the corresponding volume element at each level's local radius r[k],

dV = r[k]² cos φ dλ dφ dr ,

with horizontal arc-length spacing r[k] cos φ · Δλ / r[k] · Δφ and radial spacing Δr in the derivative stencils, and the 3×3 ambient Cartesian tensor rotation of compute_Π! keeping the radial direction.

On a spheroid the distance is the geodesic's (Karney 2013), and a vector is filtered by its ambient Cartesian components. The top-hat's support on the surface is the geodesic disk, whose edge FlowGeometries' direct geodesic problem gives at a set of azimuths, its half-width in longitude held as a Chebyshev series in latitude. A circle of geodesic radius R bounds the disk while R < πb, the distance to the nearest cut locus; no two points lie farther apart than half a meridian, and a top-hat that wide covers the whole surface. A top-hat of radius between the two raises. A (λ, φ, h) grid of geodetic heights filters over the ball of chord radius R, each cell's fraction integrated over its heights.

Filtering vectors and tensors on the sphere

Filtering each local (east, north) component of a vector as a scalar does not commute with the surface derivatives on S², and filtering its ambient Cartesian components does not either (Aluie 2019, §3 and eq. 41): the Cartesian components of the gradient ∇*Y_n of a degree-n harmonic are harmonics of degrees n − 1 and n + 1, which a radial kernel scales by Ĝ(n − 1) and Ĝ(n + 1), and the filtered field gains a radial component. Aluie (2019) builds the convolution that commutes.

The generalized convolution. The Edmonds basis K^{(i)}_{n,j} (eqs. 42–45, 50–51) splits a vector field into u = u^{(1)} + u^{(2)} + u^{(3)} (eqs. 56–57), and

G →* u = G^{(1)} * u^{(1)} + G^{(2)} * u^{(2)} + G^{(3)} * u^{(3)}                        (eq. 58)

with G^{(1)} and G^{(2)} the kernels whose Legendre transforms are Ĝ(n − 1) and Ĝ(n + 1), and G^{(3)} = G (eqs. 59–61). Every vector spherical harmonic Y_{n,j}, Ψ_{n,j}, Φ_{n,j} is then an eigenfunction with eigenvalue Ĝ(n) (Lemma 1), and filtering commutes with the surface gradient, divergence and curl (Proposition 1, eqs. 148–162). A tangent field u = ∇*Φ + e_r × ∇*Ψ (eq. 192) filters to ∇*Φ̄ + e_r × ∇*Ψ̄, Φ̄ and Ψ̄ the scalar convolutions (Proposition 2): the vector is filtered as the scalars of its Helmholtz decomposition. Rank-2 tensors follow the same construction (eqs. 121–126, Lemma 2), and a symmetric tangent tensor is filtered as the scalars of its decomposition (eq. 193, Proposition 3, eq. 194). A radial component is filtered as a scalar (eq. 150), and the tangent vector a tensor's radial row forms is filtered as a vector (eqs. 157–158).

Spin-weighted form. With ₛY_ℓm(θ, φ) = N_ℓ d^ℓ_{m,−s}(θ) e^{imφ} the spin-weighted harmonics (N_ℓ = √((2ℓ+1)/4π), d^ℓ the Wigner small-d, θ the colatitude) and λ_ℓ = √(ℓ(ℓ+1)), the identities ∂_β d^ℓ_{m,0} = (λ_ℓ/2)(d^ℓ_{m,−1} − d^ℓ_{m,1}) and (m/sin β) d^ℓ_{m,0} = −(λ_ℓ/2)(d^ℓ_{m,−1} + d^ℓ_{m,1}) give

(∂_θ + i csc θ ∂_φ) Y_ℓm = λ_ℓ ₁Y_ℓm .

For u = ∇*f + e_r × ∇*g on the unit sphere, then, u_θ + i u_φ = Σ λ_ℓ (f_ℓm + i g_ℓm) ₁Y_ℓm: a spin-1 field whose degree-ℓ coefficients are λ_ℓ(f_ℓm + i g_ℓm). f and g are real, so their coefficients obey f_ℓ,−m = (−1)^m conj(f_ℓm), and the coefficients at ±m determine f_ℓm and g_ℓm. Scaling f and g by Ĝ(ℓ), Proposition 2, is therefore scaling each degree-ℓ spin-1 coefficient by Ĝ(ℓ).

A symmetric tangent tensor T is its trace, a scalar, and its traceless part, which T(m, m) = T_ee − T_nn + 2i T_en, m = e_e + i e_n, holds whole. The traceless parts of Proposition 3, ∇*∇*f − ½(∇²f) I and its rotation by 45°, have T(m, m) proportional to the spin-2 field ð²(f + ig), whose degree-ℓ coefficients are √((ℓ−1)ℓ(ℓ+1)(ℓ+2)) (f_ℓm + i g_ℓm); scaling them by Ĝ(ℓ) is again Proposition 3. A symmetric tensor has no antisymmetric part. In the local frame U = u_e + i u_n and Q = T_ee − T_nn + 2i T_en differ from the (θ, φ) forms by constant phases, u_θ + i u_φ = iU and T(e_θ + i e_φ, e_θ + i e_φ) = −Q, which a real Ĝ(ℓ) commutes with.

The generalized convolution is, then: analyze U at spin 1, Q at spin 2 and the trace at spin 0, multiply every degree-ℓ coefficient by Ĝ(ℓ), and synthesize. A vertical velocity w is a scalar and wU a spin-1 field. A flux sweep analyzes its fields once and synthesizes them at every scale.

Transforms. Filtering.GeneralizedConvolution runs FastSphericalHarmonics' spin transforms on a Float64 Fejer1Sampling grid of 2N − 1 longitudes by N latitudes, exact for a field of degree below N; NUFSHT's on every other spherical grid where it and a NUFFT library are loaded; and otherwise the same sums evaluated directly, the Wigner d from their three-term recurrence in ℓ: on a latitude–longitude StructuredGrid by rows (Filtering.RingSpinTransforms), whose cells share a colatitude per row, so the sums along the rows are one product with e^{imφ_i} and the recurrence runs once per row, in O(M·ℓ_max + n_lat·ℓ_max²) for M cells; on any other grid cell by cell (Filtering.DirectSpinTransforms), in O(M·ℓ_max²). Its seed d^{|m|}_{m,n} ≈ sin^{|m|} θ falls below the floating-point range where ℓ_max · sin θ · log₁₀(1/sin θ) exceeds the decimal exponent range, about ℓ_max ≈ 240 in Float32 and 1900 in Float64, for orders that still reach O(ℓ^{−1/2}) past the turning point ℓ sin θ = |m|; the recurrence carries such a value as a significand and its own binary exponent. NUFSHT's and the direct transforms take a field's coefficients as analysis says (Filtering.AbstractAnalysis). Quadrature() is the sum Σ_k (A_k/R²) F_k conj(ₛY_ℓm(x_k)) over the grid's cells, exact for a field of degree at most ℓ_max where the measure integrates degree 2ℓ_max exactly (Gauss–Legendre rows), and over a regional grid the integral of the field extended by zero. LeastSquares() minimizes Σ_k A_k |F_k − Σ C_ℓm ₛY_ℓm(x_k)|² by LSMR in the inner product of the cell measures, starting from the quadrature, and recovers a field of degree at most ℓ_max wherever the cells determine its coefficients; a fit that stops at its iteration limit keeps its last iterate and reports through Filtering.analysis_status. AutoAnalysis(), the default, keeps the quadrature on Gauss–Legendre and reduced Gaussian samplings, where it is exact, and on grids that may be regional (no sampling, latitude–longitude, scattered), and fits on every other spherical sampling, whose quadrature is not exact at its band limit (Fejér, Driscoll–Healy, McEwen–Wiaux, HEALPix, cubed sphere, icosahedral, Yin–Yang); a latitude–longitude grid over the whole sphere takes LeastSquares() by name. ℓ_max is the band limit of a sampling that ties one to its ring count, and on any other grid the degree whose half wavelength is the side √A of the largest cell, ⌈πR/√A⌉ − 1 (Filtering.spin_band_limit); GeneralizedConvolution(; lmax) sets it, and the vector filter cuts sharply there. A method = Spectral() plan over a spherical node set takes analysis the same way. The transforms run in the grid's element type; the libraries run in host memory, through which a device workspace stages its fields, and the direct sum runs in the workspace's own memory under its execution backend.

Masks and regional grids. Under ZeroFill the analysis reads zero at an inactive cell and nothing past a regional grid's edges: the result is the generalized convolution of the field extended by zero over the whole sphere. Deformable renormalizes each window over its active cells, which is no convolution, and under it a vector is filtered by its ambient Cartesian components, Filtering.CartesianComponents. These equal the generalized convolution on a non-divergent tangent field, whose Cartesian components of degree n are degree-n harmonics, and filter the degree-n content of a divergent part with Ĝ(n ± 1).

Choosing. Every diagnostic that filters a vector or a tensor takes vector_filter (Filtering.resolve_vector_filter) and transform. Under the generalized convolution a scalar that combines with a filtered vector in the same diagnostic (θ in the tracer flux, ρ and P in the Favre budget) is filtered at spin 0 by the same transforms and the same Ĝ(ℓ), so the diagnostic reads one operator: a uniform tracer carries no flux and a constant density gives ρΠ, both to round-off. A scalar-only filter keeps the filter plan's engine.

On a plane and in a volume, whose kernel is a ball of the ambient space, the convolution of each Cartesian component is the generalized convolution. transform names the spin transforms' library: AutoSpectralBackend() takes the fastest loaded one, and DirectSumSpectralBackend() the direct sum.

A great-circle kernel is homogeneous in distance, and ∂/∂x is taken along λ, whose metric factor h_λ = R cos φ varies across the footprint, so even for a scalar filter ∘ ∂/∂x ≠ ∂/∂x ∘ filter on the sphere, by an amount that grows with ℓ/R and |φ| and is round-off while the footprint spans one latitude row. ∂f/∂x is a component of the vector ∇*f, and Proposition 1 is the commuting statement, ∇*(G * f) = G →* (∇*f). The filtered budget reads derivatives of filtered fields (S̄ from ū, ∇θ̄) and the generalized convolution of the velocity and the stress, which commutes with the divergence.

Curvilinear & unstructured grids: WLSQ gradients

CurvilinearGrid and UnstructuredGrid have no fixed axis spacing to difference against, so the gradient (FG.Operators.gradient_plan + FG.Operators.gradient!, which returns both tangent components from one neighbour sweep) is reconstructed from a local weighted-least-squares (WLSQ) fit over each point's neighbor stencil (its 4 index-offset neighbors on a curvilinear mesh; its k-d tree neighbors on a scattered point cloud), projected into the local tangent plane (project_to_tangent_plane, an exact 3D-chord projection on spherical grids). WLSQ gradients are conditionally second order, degrading toward first order on strongly skewed local stencils.
