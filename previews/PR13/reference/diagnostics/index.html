Diagnostics

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.AbstractSpectrumPolicy — Type
AbstractSpectrumPolicy

What to do when a filtering spectral density is asked for with a kernel whose |Ĝ(k)|² is not monotone decreasing — AutoSpectrum, StrictSpectrum, ForceSpectrum or NoSpectrum.

Sadek & Aluie (2018) eq. (21) guarantees Ẽ(k_ℓ) ≥ 0 when d|Ĝ(k)|²/dk ≤ 0, a sufficient condition. TopHatKernel's |Ĝ|² falls to zero at kℓ ≈ 7.66 and rises to 0.0175 at kℓ ≈ 10.27: its violation lies in the sub-filter tail, below 2% of the DC value.

Π and the cumulative energy carry no such condition and are unaffected by this choice.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.band_energies — Method
band_energies(u, v, w, grid, kernel, scales; maps=false, filter_plans=nothing, execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill(), method=nothing, vector_filter=nothing, transform=AutoSpectralBackend())
    -> (; bands, resolved, total, band_maps, resolved_map)

Split the kinetic energy into contributions from each scale band, using the repeated-filter generalization of the Germano identity.

With scales in ascending order ℓ₁ < ℓ₂ < … < ℓ_N, define the repeatedly filtered fields

f₀ = u ,   f_n = G_{ℓ_n} * f_{n-1} ,

so f_n has had every scale below ℓ_n removed, successively. Band n holds the energy the n-th application removed, which is the generalized second moment at that level:

k_n = ½ τ_{ℓ_n}(f_{n-1}; f_{n-1}) = ½[ (|f_{n-1}|²)‾_{ℓ_n} − |f_n|² ] ,

and the decomposition is exact:

½⟨|u|²⟩ = Σ_{n=1}^N ⟨k_n⟩ + ½⟨|f_N|²⟩ .

The sum telescopes because each ⟨G * x⟩ = ⟨x⟩: the filter conserves the integral over the grid its output is read on. Under ZeroFill every cell of that grid is summed, land included, and divided by the water area, so on a periodic grid, masked or not, the identity holds to the accuracy the discrete kernel conserves its mass. Past a bounded edge the filtered field leaves the grid, and the identity carries a residual, ½ Σₙ (⟨(|f_{n-1}|²)‾⟩ − ⟨|f_{n-1}|²⟩), of order ℓ/L. Deformable renormalizes over the active cells, which does not conserve the integral.

On a grid of non-Cartesian geometry f_n is the filtered velocity compute_Π! uses. On the surface of a sphere vector_filter (Filtering.resolve_vector_filter) filters it: by default the generalized convolution of Aluie (2019), which multiplies the degree-n part of f and of the scalar |f|² by the same Ĝ(n), its spin transforms chosen by transform; it reads no scalar filter plan, and none is built for it. Filtering.CartesianComponents(), and every spheroid, takes the local part of the filtered ambient Cartesian components of f_{n-1} and filters |f|² as a scalar.

The second-moment form has no cross terms between bands (Aluie & Eyink 2009). Under a scalar or Cartesian-component convolution k_n ≥ 0 pointwise iff the kernel is non-negative: use a positive kernel (TopHatKernel, GaussianKernel, SmoothHatKernel, HyperGaussianKernel); a signed one such as Kernels.HighOrderKernel can give negative band energies. The generalized convolution filters the parts of f with the spectrally shifted kernels of its eqs. 59–60, which may take either sign, so its map k_n may too. On the whole sphere its average is proportional to Σ_l (1 − Ĝ(l)²)|f̂_l|² over the degree-l parts f̂_l of f_{n-1}, non-negative when every |Ĝ(l)| ≤ 1, which a non-negative kernel meets.

maps = true also returns the N per-band maps in band_maps, which is otherwise nothing; resolved_map is always returned. filter_plans accepts a prebuilt sweep family. The maps live in the memory of the plans' backend, whose loops compute them.

Returns the per-band domain-averaged energies bands (length N), the energy left in f_N (resolved), their sum total, and the corresponding pointwise maps.

References

  • Germano, M. (1992). J. Fluid Mech. 238, eq. (33).
  • Aluie, H., & Eyink, G. L. (2009). Localness of energy cascade in hydrodynamic turbulence. Phys. Fluids 21, 115108, Appendix 2.

compressible_flux(u, v, ρ, P, grid::AbstractGrid{<:SphericalGeometry}, kernel, scale; vector_filter=nothing, transform=AutoSpectralBackend(), ...)

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.compressible_flux — Method
compressible_flux(u, v, ρ, P, grid, kernel, scale; execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill())
    -> (; Π, Λ, pressure_dilatation, ρ̄, P̄, ũ, ṽ)

The variable-density (Favre) cross-scale energy budget of Aluie (2013). Returns the three terms of that budget which act on the large-scale kinetic energy ρ̄|ũ|²/2, plus the filtered fields they are built from.

Favre filtering

f̃ ≡ (ρf)‾/ρ̄ is the density-weighted filter, under which the filtered continuity equation closes exactly, ∂_tρ̄ + ∂_i(ρ̄ũ_i) = 0. It is linear but does not commute with derivatives, and the budget reads both filters.

The three terms

Π = −ρ̄ ∂_j ũ_i τ̃(u_i,u_j) ,   τ̃(u_i,u_j) = (ρu_iu_j)‾/ρ̄ − ũ_iũ_j        deformation work
Λ = (1/ρ̄) ∂_j P̄ · τ̄(ρ,u_j) ,  τ̄(ρ,u_j)   = (ρu_j)‾ − ρ̄ū_j              baropycnal work
                                                                        (τ̄ unweighted)
P̄ ∇·ū                                                                   pressure dilatation

All three are per unit volume, W m⁻³ with ρ in kg m⁻³ and P in Pa; at a constant ρ₀, Π is ρ₀ times the specific flux compute_Π! returns.

Π and Λ pair a large-scale field with small-scale fluctuations and transfer energy across scales. P̄∇·ū involves the large scales alone: a conversion between kinetic and internal energy at the resolved scale. The pressure term takes the unweighted divergence; the form P̄∇·ũ plus a transport term absorbs Λ.

Masked and bounded grids

Under ZeroFill a masked cell and the domain exterior hold no fluid, so ρ̄ is the fluid mass under the kernel: the Favre fields are density-weighted means over the fluid, zero where none lies under the kernel. Constant ρ collapses the budget to ρ·Π wherever the fluid fills the kernel, which under Deformable is every active cell. ρ is read, and must be positive, on active cells only.

Asymptotics

For a smooth field, Lees & Aluie (2019) give Λ ≈ (C₂ℓ²/ρ̄)·c_d·[∇P̄·S̄·∇ρ̄ + ½ ω̄·(∇ρ̄ × ∇P̄)] with C₂ the kernel's second moment: a strain-generation part and a baroclinic part present in solenoidal flow too. Both vanish with C₂, as for the kernels of Kernels.HighOrderKernel.

References

  • Aluie, H. (2013). Scale decomposition in compressible turbulence. Physica D 247, 54–65.
  • Lees, A., & Aluie, H. (2019). Baropycnal work: a mechanism for energy transfer across scales. Fluids 4, 92. doi:10.3390/fluids4020092

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.compute_Π! — Method
compute_Π!(Π, u, v, w, grid, kernel, scale; workspace=nothing, filter_plan=nothing, execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill(), method=RealSpace(), vector_filter=nothing, transform=AutoSpectralBackend(), deriv_plan=nothing, analyzed=nothing)

The cross-scale kinetic energy flux Π = −S̄_ij τ_ij at filter scale scale (Aluie, Hecht & Vallis 2018): positive where energy passes from scales larger than scale to smaller ones.

S̄_ij = ½(∂ū_i/∂x_j + ∂ū_j/∂x_i) is the strain rate of the filtered velocity and τ_ij = (u_i u_j)‾ − ū_i ū_j the subfilter stress. On a sphere the velocity ū and the stress (u_i u_j)‾ are filtered by vector_filter (Filtering.resolve_vector_filter): by default the generalized convolution of Aluie (2019) under ZeroFill, which commutes with the surface derivatives, its spin transforms chosen by transform; Filtering.CartesianComponents() filters the ambient Cartesian components and rotates them back to the local frame. vector_filter and transform build the workspace when workspace is not given. The generalized convolution reads no scalar filter plan, and none is built for it when filter_plan is not given.

u and v are the eastward and northward components. A given w is the vertical velocity of a thin layer: S_xz = ½∂w̄/∂x, S_yz = ½∂w̄/∂y and S_zz = 0, with no vertical derivative, the thin-layer scaling of Vallis (2017, §5) and Pedlosky (1987, ch. 6). Vertical structure is this method applied level by level (Aluie, Hecht & Vallis 2018; Buzzicotti et al. 2023), as Pipeline.coarse_grain_profile does; a 3-D grid takes the method that differentiates along all three axes.

Fields may carry trailing batch axes past the grid's two. Under mask_strategy = ZeroFill() the kernel is the same at every position, so filtering commutes with derivatives; Filtering.filter_field! describes each strategy at a boundary. execution, mask_strategy and method build the filter plan when filter_plan is not given. A prebuilt filter_plan, deriv_plan (Derivatives.derivative_plan(output_grid(grid, mask_strategy))) and workspace serve repeated calls; the workspace lives in the memory of the plan's backend (ΠWorkspace) and the flux's own loops run under the workspace's. Π and the fields live there too. analyzed takes a sweep's analyze_sweep result.

Returns Π, the specific flux in m² s⁻³ (velocity in m s⁻¹, lengths in m); ρ₀Π is the flux per unit volume, W m⁻³.

Π = zeros(100, 100)

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.compute_Π_decomposed — Method
compute_Π_decomposed(u, v, u_rot, v_rot, grid, kernel, scale; execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill())
    -> (; total, rotational, cross, divergent)

Split the cross-scale flux Π = −S̄:τ on a plane into rotational–rotational Π_RR, divergent–divergent Π_DD and interaction Π_X parts, the last the stimulated-cascade channel of Barkan, Srinivasan & McWilliams (2024), by splitting both sides of the contraction.

(u_rot, v_rot) is the rotational (divergence-free) part of (u, v), and the divergent part is the rest, (u, v) − (u_rot, v_rot). With u = uʳ + uᵈ the strain is linear, S̄ = S̄ʳ + S̄ᵈ, and the stress bilinear, τ(u,u) = τʳʳ + τᵈᵈ + τ_X with τ_X = τ(uʳ,uᵈ) + τ(uᵈ,uʳ), so

Π_RR = −S̄ʳ:τʳʳ
Π_DD = −S̄ᵈ:τᵈᵈ
Π_X  = −(S̄ʳ:τᵈᵈ + S̄ᵈ:τʳʳ + S̄ʳ:τ_X + S̄ᵈ:τ_X)

and Π = Π_RR + Π_X + Π_DD exactly, each channel built from its own terms.

Returns specific flux maps (m² s⁻³): rotational = ΠRR, divergent = ΠDD, cross = Π_X.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.compute_Π_strain_convergence — Method
compute_Π_strain_convergence(u, v, grid, kernel, scale; execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill())
    -> (; total, strain, convergence, divergence, strain_magnitude)

Split the 2D cross-scale flux into the two production terms of Srinivasan, Barkan & McWilliams (2023), eq. (10), by diagonalizing the filtered strain tensor. With

δ̄   = ū_x + v̄_y            divergence         (rotation invariant)
σ̄_n = ū_x − v̄_y            normal strain
σ̄_s = ū_y + v̄_x            shear strain
ᾱ   = √(σ̄_n² + σ̄_s²)       strain magnitude   (rotation invariant)

the flux separates into

Π = Π_α − Π_δ ,   Π_α = (τ_vv − τ_uu) σ̄_n/2 − τ_uv σ̄_s ,   Π_δ = (τ_vv + τ_uu) δ̄/2 ,

with Π_α the deformation (shear) production, energy transferred by straining, and Π_δ the convergence production, zero for a non-divergent field. Setting δ̄ = 0 recovers Polzin (2010); the equivalent Π = E′(γᵖ ᾱ − δ̄) form with E′ = (τ_vv + τ_uu)/2 recovers Jing et al. (2017), and |γᵖ| ≤ 1 gives the bound |Π_α| ≤ ᾱ E′.

total = Π_α − Π_δ equals the Π = −S̄:τ̄ of compute_Π!: both expand to −τ_uu ū_x − τ_uv(ū_y + v̄_x) − τ_vv v̄_y.

Returns specific flux maps (m² s⁻³) and the two rotation invariants, divergence = δ̄ and strain_magnitude = ᾱ.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.enstrophy_flux — Method
enstrophy_flux(u, v, grid, kernel, scale; execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill(), vector_filter=nothing, transform=AutoSpectralBackend()) -> Z

Cross-scale enstrophy flux (Rivera, Aluie & Ecke 2014, eq. 16),

Z_ℓ = −∂_j ω̄_ℓ · τ_ℓ(u_j, ω) ,   τ_j = (u_j ω)‾ − ū_j ω̄ ,   ω = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y ,

the enstrophy analogue of compute_Π!: positive means enstrophy moving to smaller scales.

Gauge

This is the deformation (subtracted) form, the gauge Π uses: the resolved product ū_j ω̄ is subtracted, and the flux is pointwise Galilean-invariant. The unsubtracted −∂_j ω̄ (u_j ω)‾ differs from it by a transport divergence; the two share a spatial mean on a homogeneous domain and "differ qualitatively as well as quantitatively" on an inhomogeneous or masked one (Aluie 2011; Aluie, Hecht & Vallis 2018).

Z is tracer_variance_flux with θ = ω from vorticity!; on a sphere vector_filter and transform choose how it filters the vector u ω.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.filtering_spectrum — Method
filtering_spectrum(u, v, w, grid, kernel, scales; L=1, workspace=nothing, filter_plans=nothing, execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill(), method=RealSpace(), vector_filter=nothing, transform=AutoSpectralBackend(), policy=StrictSpectrum())
    -> (k_ℓ, Ẽ)

Filtering spectral density (Sadek & Aluie 2018, PRF, Eq. 14): the derivative of the cumulative coarse-grained KE (cumulative_energy, Eq. 15) with respect to the filtering wavenumber k_ℓ = L/ℓ,

Ẽ(k_ℓ) = d/dk_ℓ [ ½⟨|ū_ℓ|²⟩ ] = -(ℓ²/L) d/dℓ[ ½⟨|ū_ℓ|²⟩ ].

scales need not be uniform. Returns the filtering wavenumbers k_ℓ and the density Ẽ per scale.

Wavenumber convention

k_ℓ = L/ℓ with L the region length is the Sadek–Aluie convention: in their Fourier series f(x) = Σ_k f̂(k) e^{i(2π/L)k·x}, k is a dimensionless index. The default L = 1 gives k_ℓ = 1/ℓ, the convention of Storer et al. (2022, 2023); L = 2π gives k_ℓ = 2π/ℓ (Rivera, Aluie & Ecke 2014).

Under k_ℓ = C/ℓ the density carries the Jacobian dℓ/dk_ℓ = -ℓ²/C, so Ẽ scales as 1/C and k_ℓ as C: amplitudes and peak locations compare across spectra only under one convention. cumulative_energy is independent of C.

Limits

  • Slope ceiling. Sadek & Aluie eq. (18): a kernel with p vanishing moments recovers a k^{-α} spectrum only for α < p + 2, and otherwise saturates at k^{-(p+2)}. TopHatKernel and GaussianKernel have p = 1, so the measured slope saturates at k⁻³, the enstrophy-range slope of 2-D and QG turbulence. The flux Π has no such ceiling.
  • Kernel admissibility. Ẽ(k_ℓ) ≥ 0 is guaranteed only when d|Ĝ(k)|²/dk ≤ 0. By default this function throws for a kernel that fails it; pass policy = ForceSpectrum() to compute it anyway. See AbstractSpectrumPolicy and Kernels.transfer_monotone.

References

  • Sadek & Aluie (2018), Phys. Rev. Fluids 3, 124610.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.tau_decomposition — Method
tau_decomposition(u, v, grid, kernel, scale; execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill())
    -> (; L, C, R)

Split the 2D subfilter-scale stress τ_ij = ⟨u_i u_j⟩ - ū_i ū_j into Leonard, Cross, and Reynolds contributions (Germano 1992, JFM 238); each is a generalized central moment and is Galilean-invariant. With ū = G * u the filtered velocity and u' = u - ū the residual, and the generalized second moment M(f, g) = (fg)‾ - f̄ ḡ:

  • Leonard L_ij = M(ū_i, ū_j) (resolved–resolved),

  • Reynolds R_ij = M(u'_i, u'_j) (subfilter–subfilter; backscatter),

with L + C + R = τ exactly. Returns a named tuple of named tuples, each holding the symmetric 2D components (; xx, xy, yy) as arrays.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.tracer_variance_flux — Method
tracer_variance_flux(u, v, θ, grid, kernel, scale; execution=AutoBackend(), mask_strategy=ZeroFill())
    -> Πθ

Cross-scale flux of the tracer variance ½⟨θ'²⟩ at filter scale ℓ (the scalar analog of the kinetic energy flux Π; Aluie & Eyink):

Πθ(x) = -∂_j θ̄ · τ_j(u, θ),   τ_j = ⟨u_j θ⟩ - ū_j θ̄  (the subfilter tracer flux),

with the same sign convention as compute_Π!: Πθ > 0 is a forward cascade of tracer variance toward small scales, Πθ < 0 an inverse cascade.

With θ the buoyancy b = -g ρ'/ρ₀ this is the cross-scale transfer of buoyancy variance, tied to available potential energy. This method serves a plane; a sphere and a volume have their own. The result lives in the memory of execution.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.tracer_variance_flux — Method
tracer_variance_flux(u, v, θ, grid::AbstractGrid{<:SphericalGeometry}, kernel, scale; vector_filter=nothing, transform=AutoSpectralBackend(), ...) -> Πθ

The tracer-variance flux on a sphere, on any grid resolving two tangent directions — structured, curvilinear, a scattered node set or a sphere pixelization. The velocity and the vector uθ of the subfilter flux τ_j = ⟨u_j θ⟩ - ū_j θ̄ are filtered by vector_filter (Filtering.resolve_vector_filter). By default that is the generalized convolution of Aluie (2019), whose spin transforms transform chooses; θ̄ is then their spin-0 transform with the same Ĝ(n), so the flux reads one operator, and the filter plan is not built. Filtering.CartesianComponents() forms τ in ambient Cartesian components and rotates it to the local (east, north) frame, where its radial component drops, and the filter plan filters θ. τ is contracted against ∂_j θ̄.

CoarseGrainingEnergyFluxes.Diagnostics.vorticity! — Method
vorticity!(ω, u, v, grid[, deriv_plan]; scratch = nothing, execution = SerialBackend()) -> ω

The vertical component of the relative vorticity on a grid resolving two tangent directions — structured, curvilinear, a scattered node set or a sphere pixelization — zero on masked cells: the curl in the local frame, ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y on a plane and

ζ = (1/(R cos φ))[∂v/∂λ − ∂(u cos φ)/∂φ] = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y + u tan φ/R

on a sphere, with ∂/∂x, ∂/∂y derivatives per unit distance. A cell on a pole is zero. scratch is two arrays the size of ω; with it and deriv_plan supplied, a call allocates nothing. The arrays live in the memory of execution, whose loops take the curl; grid and deriv_plan are moved there.
